What the preset decides

Three rules

01

A signal must be legible

The specified red fails as text, under white and under the ink. In a control panel the red is the one colour that cannot be decorative — if it cannot be read, it is not a warning.

02

Two shadows, never one

A dark shadow one way and a white highlight the other. One shadow alone is a card lying on a page; the pair is a surface moulded out of the page itself.

03

Values are monospace

Specifications, readouts, prices, status. The split between Inter and the mono is functional rather than decorative, and it is what gives the page its instrument quality.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this a mock-up or the real thing?
A specimen, hand-built to the preset's specification so the style can be seen whole rather than as a thumbnail. The preset itself is a set of WordPress Theme Settings, and a generated site carries the chassis colour, the alert red, Inter over JetBrains Mono, the 12px radius and the two-shadow extrusion with its pressed state. The recessed wells and the embossed hero type are markup, so a section that wants them still asks for them.
Why is the red darker than the specification?
Because the specified red measures 2.64 to one as text on this chassis. A pale grey ground is unforgiving: a colour that looks vivid on a dark mock-up loses almost all of its separation here.
Does the extrusion work without shadows?
No — and that is the honest limitation of the style. An extruded control has no border and no fill difference, so in a forced-colours mode or in print it disappears. Anything genuinely interactive needs a boundary underneath the moulding.
