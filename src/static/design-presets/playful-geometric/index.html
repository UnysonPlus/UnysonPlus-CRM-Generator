What the preset decides

Three rules

1

The brand colour had to move

White on the specification's violet is 4.23 to one and the slate on it is 3.45 — it fails in both directions, so no label rescues it. One step darker fixes the fill, the link and the text colour at the same time.

2

Slate on every confetti colour

Pink, amber and mint all fail under white and all pass comfortably under the slate. They are fills behind shapes, and when they do carry text, the text is dark.

3

The decoration stays outside

Shapes live beside the content column, never across it. That is the whole difference between this and a page that merely looks busy.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this a mock-up or the real thing?
A specimen, hand-built to the preset's specification so the style can be seen whole rather than as a thumbnail. The preset itself is a set of WordPress Theme Settings, and a generated site carries the palette, the type pairing, the mixed radii and the sticker shadow from the start. The confetti shapes, the squiggles and the dot grid are markup, so a section that wants them still asks for them.
Why is the violet not the one in the specification?
Because that violet is the rare case where a fill fails in both directions at once: 4.23 to one under white and 3.45 under the slate. There is no text colour that saves it. One step darker and the same value works as a fill, as a link and as body emphasis.
Why no soft shadows?
Because a blurred shadow makes an element look like a card lying on a page, and a hard offset makes it look like a sticker stuck to one. The second is the entire feeling of this style, and it is one CSS value away from being lost.
