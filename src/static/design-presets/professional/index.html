What the preset decides

Three rules

One gold, dark enough

It has to read as a link on the ivory and carry a white label as a fill. The specified value does neither; this one does both at a little under five to one.

Warm hairlines

A neutral grey rule beside a warm ivory page makes the page look like a mistake. The border colour is tinted to match, which is the sort of detail nobody notices and everybody feels.

Playfair stays regular

The face already has presence — the stroke contrast is doing the work. Bolding it turns gravitas into shouting and flattens the thin strokes that made it elegant.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this a mock-up or the real thing?
A specimen, hand-built to the preset's specification so the style can be seen whole rather than as a thumbnail. The preset itself is a set of WordPress Theme Settings, and a generated site carries the ivory, the gold, Playfair over Source Sans, the 6px radius and the warm hairlines. The ruled layouts here — a standfirst column, a rule above a card instead of a box around it — are markup, so a section that wants them still asks for them.
Why is the gold different from the specification?
Because the specified gold fails as text at 3.11 to one and under white at 3.25. That is the particular trap of a single-accent palette: the one colour has to do three jobs, so it has to pass in both directions rather than in one.
Why is almost nothing boxed?
Because a box is a heavy way to say what a rule says lightly. Cards here carry a rule above them rather than a border around them, which puts the weight at the top of the element the way a rule above a column does in print.
