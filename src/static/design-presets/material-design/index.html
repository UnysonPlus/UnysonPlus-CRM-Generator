What the preset decides

Three decisions

Pills, always

Every button fully rounded. Not "quite rounded" — a slightly-rounded rectangle reads as a different design system entirely.

State layers

Hover and press are opacity overlays rather than colour swaps, so an element keeps its identity while responding.

Tone plus elevation

The card tone is barely one-tenth of a step from the page. The shadow is what makes the boundary visible, which makes it structural rather than ornamental.

Surfaces

Tone alone is not a boundary

The container tint measures 1.12:1 against the page behind it. That is a difference you can see on a good monitor in a bright room and not much else — so the elevation shadow is doing accessibility work here, not decoration.

Which means "no border by default" has to be read as "no border in addition to elevation". A surface that loses its shadow — in a flat section, in print, in a high-contrast mode — needs the outline back.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this a mock-up or the real thing?
A specimen, hand-built to the preset's specification so the style can be seen whole rather than as a thumbnail. The preset itself is a set of WordPress Theme Settings; a generated site inherits the palette, the type scale and the radii. The blurred shapes, the state layers and the filled text field are still being folded into the preset and are not all emitted automatically yet.
Why do the cards have shadows if the system is tonal?
Because the tone on its own is 1.12:1 against the page, which is not a boundary anyone can rely on. The specification says to separate containers with tone rather than borders, and that only works because elevation is carrying the difference. Remove the shadow and the card needs its outline back.
Do the buttons have to be fully round?
Yes — it is the clearest single marker of the system. A twelve-pixel radius on a button reads as a different design language, even when every other value is correct.
