Volume I — Proclamation

On the matter of crimson

Crimson is a fill and never ink. On this mahogany ground it measures 2.05 to one, which is to say it is invisible: as a line of text, as a border, as an icon, it simply does not arrive. Set it behind parchment instead and it measures 6.57 and becomes the most confident colour on the page — the wax seal below is the same red that would have failed as a sentence.

The source specification never notices, because every use it lists happens already to be a fill. The failure waits for the first person who reaches for it as a colour for a heading.

Volume II — What the preset decides

Three findings

I

Crimson is a fill

Two point zero five to one as ink; six point five seven behind parchment. The same colour, used two ways, on either side of the line.

II

Keep the faded ink off the leather

Secondary text measures 5.40 on the canvas and 5.00 on the oak surface, but 3.74 on the worn-leather tertiary — a pair of tokens that invite each other and should not meet.

III

There is more headroom than stated

Parchment on mahogany is 13.48, not the 8.5 the specification targets. That surplus is spent here on the small engraved labels, which are the type most at risk.

Volume IV — Enquiries

Frequently asked

Is this a mock-up or the real thing?
A specimen, hand-built to the preset's specification so the style can be seen whole rather than as a thumbnail. The preset itself is a set of WordPress Theme Settings; a generated site inherits the palette, the three serifs and the 4px radius. The arch tops, the corner flourishes and the wax seal are still being folded into the preset and are not all emitted automatically yet.
Why is there so little crimson?
Because it is only safe as a fill. It appears here exactly twice — the seal on the featured tier, and the hover state of the outlined button, which fills with it and switches its text to parchment. Used as a colour for type on this canvas it measures 2.05 to one.
Why serifs for the buttons as well?
Because an engraved all-caps face is what a brass plate would carry, and a sans label on a brass button is the single detail that would give the whole thing away as a dark theme wearing a costume.
