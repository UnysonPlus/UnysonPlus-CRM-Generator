What the preset decides

Three rules

1

A near-white ground is unforgiving

All three of the specified colours fail on it — the accent in both directions, the teal badly, the muted grey as text. Soft-UI palettes tend to be picked against a mid grey and then used on a page like this one.

2

A shadow is not a boundary

An extruded control has no border and no fill difference, so it exists only while its shadows do. In a forced-colours mode it disappears entirely. Interactive elements here keep a faint outline underneath.

3

The light never moves

Top left, on every element, without exception. One element lit from the other side does not look varied — it looks broken, because the eye reads the whole page as one object.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this a mock-up or the real thing?
A specimen, hand-built to the preset's specification so the style can be seen whole rather than as a thumbnail. The preset itself is a set of WordPress Theme Settings, and a generated site carries the one surface colour, the re-measured accents, Plus Jakarta Sans over DM Sans, the 16px radius and the two-shadow extrusion with its pressed state. The recessed wells are markup, so a section that wants them still asks for them.
Why are all three accent colours different from the specification?
Because all three fail on the surface the specification itself defines — 3.41, 2.58 and 3.82 to one. That is not bad luck; it is what happens when a palette is chosen against a darker mock-up and then applied to a near-white page.
Is this style accessible at all?
With care, and with one honest caveat. Text contrast is solvable — the values here all pass. What cannot be solved by colour alone is the boundary: an element made only of shadows has none. Keeping a faint outline under anything interactive costs nothing visually and is the difference between a style and a trap.
