What the preset decides

Three rules

1

The gradient is safe here

Both stops carry white above five to one, which is unusual — most of the gradients in this set fail at their light end. That is what makes a gradient button reasonable in this style and reckless in several others.

2

The emerald is a status, not a button

White on it is 2.54 to one and the slate ink is 7.04. It belongs on a tick, a dot or a pill with dark text — never as a fill with a white label.

3

Muted text needs margin

The specified value is 4.55 to one — exactly the AA line. Darkening it slightly costs nothing and means a later change to the page tint cannot quietly push it under.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this a mock-up or the real thing?
A specimen, hand-built to the preset's specification so the style can be seen whole rather than as a thumbnail. The preset itself is a set of WordPress Theme Settings, and a generated site carries the page tint, the indigo, Plus Jakarta Sans at three weights, the 12px radius and the hairline borders. The gradient fills, the blurred orbs and the dashboard card are markup, so a section that wants them still asks for them.
Why is the emerald never a button?
Because white on it is 2.54 to one. It is the right colour for a success state and the wrong colour for anything carrying a white label, which is exactly how the specification uses it — the failure only appears when someone promotes it to an accent.
Why so few gradients?
Because a gradient signals importance by being rare. On the primary action and one headline phrase it reads as emphasis; on every heading and card it becomes the background noise it was meant to rise above.
